The opportunity

The national system reaches phones in seconds. What happens after the alert is harder.

Messages vanish

Cell broadcast text is short and gone once dismissed. People under stress need to re-read it and see when the danger ended.

Many languages

About four in ten residents are non-Saudi, and millions of pilgrims visit each year. Alert languages are not publicly documented.

Not everyone hears a siren

Deaf, blind, older and low-literacy residents need text, speech, large type and plain words.

Calls jam networks

After an alert, families phone each other. A one-tap “I’m safe” reduces load and anxiety.

Rumours spread

Fake “alerts” circulate on social media. People need a way to see what is genuinely official.

Guidance is scattered

Preparedness advice lives in posts, PDFs and videos, which are hard to reach when networks are slow.

Sources: public reporting on the National Early Warning Platform (2021–2026); General Authority for Statistics census 2022. Full list in docs/01-research.md.

These are opportunities consistent with public information, not criticisms of the official system. Every one needs validating with Civil Defense. The national platform already does the hardest part: fast, area-targeted delivery through cell broadcast, plus sirens and the Yellow, Red and White signals.
